## Investment thesis and strongest alternative

**Potential mechanism:** a resilient cash-generating brand may be discounted
alongside a troubled acquisition. Per-share value can compound if reinvestment
earns attractive returns and distributions occur below intrinsic value. A
headline earnings screen cannot decide that question.

**Competing explanation:** historical margins reflect exceptional brand demand;
cash distributions mask deteriorating underlying economics. A low earnings
multiple then reflects risk rather than mispricing. No proprietary demand data,
channel sell-through series, unit-level retention or superior consensus forecast
has been established here. This makes valuation discipline especially important.

The FY2025 segment reference is Crocs revenue $3,325.807m, segment EBIT
$1,111.679m, HEYDUDE revenue $714.840m and corporate cost $293.309m. The base
case's Crocs margin is close to that historical margin; its corporate burden is
slightly higher. HEYDUDE profitability is explicitly an assumption. (A, segment
table.) Brand-specific economics must improve in total dollars, not just EPS.

The bear is a severe impairment of franchise economics, not a likely one-year
price path. It assumes no working-capital release despite falling sales. The
base gives HEYDUDE no subsequent sales recovery. The bull requires better results
in both brands. Fixed capex and margin assumptions simplify the operating model;
they do not establish investment capacity or sustainable incremental returns.

Common assumptions: five explicit years; 12% unlevered discount rate (sensitivity
10–14%, not an estimated market WACC); 49m diluted shares; $1,310m debt-value
proxy; $100m cash retained for operations. The share allowance exceeds June basic
shares and is not an assertion of current fully diluted shares. The debt proxy
approximates reported borrowing, not verified September principal/fair value.

Tax settlements are incremental stress allowances, not predictions or deductions
of the entire tax liability. They must not duplicate ordinary forecast taxes.
Tax assets are not separately monetized. Dropping the base settlement raises
value only to $104.53; it does not change the research judgment.

## Valuation and capital allocation

For each year:

`EBIT = Crocs sales × margin + HEYDUDE sales × margin − corporate cost`

`FCFF = EBIT − operating cash taxes + D&A − capex − incremental NWC`

Cash tax has no automatic loss credit. Stock compensation remains an expense in
the modeled margins/corporate cost and is not added back. D&A excludes lease
cost addbacks. Operating lease/rent economics remain in EBIT; lease liabilities
are consequently not also subtracted as financial debt. No future dilution is
charged for the same compensation a second time.

Discount annual FCFF plus `FCFF5 × (1+g)/(r−g)` at Year 5; subtract debt and the
present value of incremental tax settlement, add cash above the operating reserve,
then divide by assumed diluted shares. Terminal growth presumes adequate ongoing
reinvestment within the cash-flow allowance; this has not been independently
proved. No historical earnings multiple supplies the terminal valuation.

These are scenario values, not a distribution of future returns. Terminal value
contributes 60.7% of base operating value. Base value at discount rates 10%, 12%
and 14% is $131.29, $102.09 and $81.91. That sensitivity is decisive: a buy case
at the reference quote relies on assumptions I cannot yet defend strongly enough.

The model forecasts unlevered base cash generation of $657m; it does not assume
that the historical $663m after-interest diagnostic repeats. Capex is higher than
the trailing reported amount, future taxes are explicit, and cash must fund the
operating balance sheet. No debt paydown is also counted as an extra return on
top of the DCF distributions.

**Buyback check:** an incremental $250m repurchase at $111.72, with an assumed
1% excise cost, reduces base intrinsic value/share from $102.09 to $101.58 after
cash cost. The 1% is a modeling assumption, not a tax-law determination. This
isolated illustration is not added to the DCF. Buying below intrinsic value can
help remaining owners; EPS accretion alone cannot establish that. Debt-financed
repurchases must likewise reduce net equity value by the financing cost.

## Decision, falsification and evidence boundary

**WATCH, with reconsideration near $82 if the base thesis survives.** The 20%
margin of safety is an explicit case-level judgment, not an inherited charter
rule, calibrated optimum or automated trading threshold. A lower quote caused
by worse franchise economics requires lower cash forecasts, not a reflexive buy.

Alternatively, reconsider at a higher price only if comparable disclosures justify
higher sustainable cash earnings with the same valuation discipline. Examine the
next two reporting quarters by 2027-03-15 for total operating profit, brand demand,
cash conversion excluding tax timing, capital spending and financing of repurchases.
Two consecutive quarters of negative Crocs-brand constant-currency revenue growth
would materially challenge the positive-growth base case. Renewed persistent
HEYDUDE losses challenge its 10% assumed margin. Rebuild, rather than preserve,
the model when these assumptions fail. Missing disclosures remain unresolved.

Main risks: permanent brand/margin erosion; excessive distributions or refinancing
costs; and taxes/working-capital/investment needs exceeding modeled allowances.
Confidence is medium in the cash reconciliation and low-to-medium in valuation;
no numerical confidence probability is claimed.

2026; vendor lineage is not exposed. Latest balance-sheet inputs remain June.
The July release's full financial tables and FY2025 filing were inspected; a
